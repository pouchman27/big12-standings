How the custom resume composite works

This is our own transparent comparison method. It is not an official ESPN metric and not the committee’s ranking; the committee’s actual order is the CFP # next to each team.

Strength of record (ESPN): ESPN’s SOR rank across all 138 FBS teams — the chance an average top-25 team would have this record or better, given the schedule. ESPN’s headline resume metric.

Game control (ESPN): chance an average top-25 team would control games from start to finish the way this team did, given the schedule.

Average in-game win % (ESPN): the team’s average in-game win probability, adjusted for how an average team would have done with the same situations.

Schedule strength (ESPN): rank of schedule difficulty over games already played, from an average top-25 team’s perspective.

ESPN resume avg (ours): the four ESPN resume-metric ranks (strength of record, game control, in-game win %, schedule strength) averaged straight, keeping each metric’s original FBS-wide scale. This is an average of ordinal ranks, not a raw rating, and it is not ESPN’s SOR itself; it does not re-rank within the 25 and does not include our big-win or bad-loss measures.

Big wins (ours): 2 points for each win over a team in the committee top 25 shown at the top of this page — by where that opponent ranks in that list today, not where it was ranked when the game was played — plus 1 point for each win over an unranked FBS opponent with a winning record.

Bad losses (ours): losses to unranked opponents — 2 points (worse) when the opponent is FCS or has a losing record, 1 point otherwise. Fewer points is better here.

Custom resume composite (ours): each team is ranked 1–25 against the others on every pillar (ties share a rank), and the six pillar ranks are averaged. It measures this group relative to itself, not against the whole FBS. Sort by any pillar instead if you weigh them differently.

Not resume items: ESPN’s FPI and efficiency ratings measure team strength, not accomplishment, so they’re shown for context in each team’s detail but don’t count in the score. Remaining SOS is forward-looking and is shown in the detail too.

Ranked means the committee’s top 25 shown at the top. Regular-season games only. All ESPN metrics refresh from its FPI feed; results and rankings from ESPN. When ESPN publishes new committee rankings, Refresh picks them up.

ESPN groups these four metrics under the resume category of its Football Power Index feed. The metric descriptions above are our plain-language summaries; every number comes straight from ESPN.
