Playoff resume compare

The committee’s top 25 compared on what they’ve actually done: strength of record, big wins, schedule strength and bad losses. Each pillar ranks the 25 against each other; the resume score averages the four pillar ranks, so a lower score is the stronger relative resume. Tap a column header to sort by it. Tap a team for its game-by-game resume.

How the resume score works

Strength of record: ESPN’s SOR rank across all 136 FBS teams — the chance an average top-25 team would have this record or better, given the schedule. The committee leans on this metric.

Big wins: 2 points for each win over a team in the committee top 25 shown at the top of this page — by where that opponent ranks in that list today, not where it was ranked when the game was played — plus 1 point for each win over an unranked FBS opponent with a winning record.

Schedule strength: ESPN’s rank of schedule difficulty over games already played, from an average top-25 team’s perspective.

Bad losses: losses to unranked opponents — 2 points (worse) when the opponent is FCS or has a losing record, 1 point otherwise. Fewer points is better here.

This is our own transparent comparison method. It is not an official ESPN metric and not the committee’s ranking; the committee’s actual order is the CFP # next to each team.

Resume score: each team is ranked 1–25 against the others on every pillar (ties share a rank), and the four pillar ranks are averaged. It measures this group relative to itself, not against the whole FBS. Sort by any pillar instead if you weigh them differently.

Ranked means the committee’s top 25 shown at the top. Regular-season games only. Strength-of-record and schedule ranks refresh from ESPN’s FPI feed; results and rankings from ESPN. When ESPN publishes new committee rankings, Refresh picks them up.
