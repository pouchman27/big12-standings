Monte Carlo simulation: the win-out, played forward

A hybrid scenario model, not a full joint Monte Carlo: the Big 12 race is simulated in full - real standings, real remaining schedule, the conference's official tiebreakers - while every other conference's champion is a heuristic draw (see the fine print), not a simulation of that league. Thousands of simulated rest-of-seasons in which BYU beats Baylor, Kansas and Cincinnati; every tracked game is drawn once per simulation, so within what is tracked each world stays consistent: when Texas beats LSU in a world, LSU takes the loss in that world.

Simulations
Champion-draw consistency
What the simulation does. BYU's three remaining games are forced wins (that is the scenario). Every other remaining game involving a CFP top-25 team is drawn from ESPN's matchup-predictor probability for that game (fetched Nov 11), including all 24 remaining Big 12 conference games. The Big 12's two championship-game spots come out of actual conference records plus the official tiebreaker order (head-to-head, record vs tied teams, common opponents, record vs the next-placed teams, opponent win rate, overall record); the steps the policy leaves to SportSource analytics or a coin toss are not computable, so those ties resolve randomly and count. The title game itself is a neutral-site draw on ESPN FPI ratings (same logistic curve as the Big 12 simulator). Other conferences: champions are NOT played out from standings - games between two non-contenders are not tracked, so full SEC, Big Ten and ACC tables are never computed and no drawn champion is verified eligible for its conference title game. Instead the champion is a heuristic draw: ESPN's FPI conference-title odds, re-weighted by that world's simulated results, with each simulated loss cutting a contender's title weight by the factor set in the control above (default 10x). Every contender's own remaining games are tracked (3 per team), so the draw reacts to each contender's actual simulated results - but a contender that loses twice can still be drawn, because two losses do not make a conference title impossible, just unlikely; the factor is your adjustable judgment call, not a sportsbook or committee claim. For scale: with no re-weighting, 27% of worlds crown an SEC, Big Ten or ACC champion that lost 2 or more of its remaining games in that same world; at 10x that is about 1%. The title-game loser is drawn from the same re-weighted odds - an approximation, since FPI publishes title-win odds, not runner-up odds. The Group of 6 automatic bid goes to the highest-ranked Group of 6 team in each world's ranking, champion or not (2026-27 format); Group of 6 conference champions are drawn the same re-weighted way and unranked Group of 6 teams slot in by FPI rating - labeled approximations. Remaining games of unranked teams with live title odds use the FPI rating logistic. Games are drawn independently; the model knows nothing about injuries, weather or momentum.
What it cannot know. How the actual committee will rank teams. The final ranking is approximated by the rule you pick above - a transparent stand-in, not a prediction of committee behavior. Switch rules and watch the buckets move; that spread is the honest uncertainty. Playoff format follows the published 2026-27 rules: ACC, Big Ten, Big 12 and SEC champions are automatic bids; the highest-ranked Group of 6 team gets a bid whether or not it wins its conference; Notre Dame gets a bid if it finishes in the top 12; the field is then seeded straight by committee ranking, top four seeds get byes, 5-8 host first-round games, 9-12 travel.
Sources: CFP committee rankings (Nov 10); ESPN matchup-predictor probabilities and FPI ratings/title odds (fetched Nov 11); Big 12 standings and schedule from ESPN (Nov 11); format per NCAA.com. The simulation uses a fixed random seed, so re-running with the same settings reproduces the same numbers. ESPN's branch numbers above come from a different model; nothing here is calibrated to them, or vice versa.

Method and sources

Win-out scenario branches: ESPN Allstate Playoff Predictor - their words: "There are 200,000 simulations," updated Nov 11, 2026. BYU was picked to beat Baylor, Kansas and Cincinnati; the three branches are the predictor's outputs for BYU winning, losing, or missing the Big 12 title game. Whole-percent values are kept exactly as displayed. Appendix simulation numbers: cfb.fyi 2026 playoff projection - their words: "We played out the rest of the season 10,000 times using each game's betting line and team power ratings," updated Nov 11, 2026. Their output is final bracket seeds (with automatic bids applied), not committee-rank movement; no source we found publishes the pairwise odds of one specific team passing another, so those aren't on this page. Seed bands: bye = seeds 1-4, home = 5-8, road = 9-12, out = unseeded. Bid-thief math: ESPN FPI conference-title odds (Nov 9 snapshot) over the Nov 10 CFP rankings; exact thief counts combine the four per-conference probabilities with an independence assumption, consistent with the ≈70% at-least-one figure. These are model outputs, not committee predictions; the committee decides the real rankings every Tuesday.
